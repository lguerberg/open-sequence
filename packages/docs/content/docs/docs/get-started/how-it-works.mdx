## One contact's life, in ten lines

1. Your app upserts a sender (`PUT /v1/senders/u_42`) and connects their Google account (`POST /v1/senders/u_42/connections/google`): one consent, Gmail and Calendar.
2. Your app upserts a contact with an email, a time zone and some `attrs` (`PUT /v1/contacts/p_7`).
3. Someone publishes a sequence version: an email, a two-day wait, a condition, a text, a call task.
4. Your app enrolls the contact as that sender (`POST /v1/sequences/seq_x/enrollments`). The service refuses if the contact is already in any sequence.
5. The planner sees the enrollment is due, runs the pure interpreter, and queues the email step.
6. The email executor checks the gates (suppressions, channel caps, send window), gets a fresh access token from the auth module, sends through the sender's Gmail with its own `Message-ID`, records the message and the run, and schedules the wait.
7. Two business days later the planner evaluates the condition: it asks Attio for the contact's open deal and reads its stage, records the facts it read, and takes a branch.
8. The text step is skipped (`no_consent`) because the contact never opted in; the skip is recorded and the flow continues.
9. The call step creates a task and pushes the number into the sender's Allo dialer; when Allo reports the call, the task completes itself with the outcome, which wakes the flow.
10. The contact replies to the first email, or books a slot that lands in the sender's Google Calendar. The signal is attributed to the email step, the enrollment exits with `replied` or `meeting_booked`, and a webhook `enrollment.exited` reaches your app.

## Three loops

```text
scheduler (lease rows, every few seconds)
   │
   ├─ loop 1: planner ── claim due enrollments, fair per sender, advance ──► queue seq_email, seq_sms
   │
   ├─ loop 2: executors and pollers ── send through the sender's accounts, poll Gmail and Calendar, check health
   │
   └─ loop 3: signals and webhooks ── process signals (exit, pause, wake, attribute) ──► queue seq_webhooks ──► your endpoints
                                                                                                    ▲
   api: provider webhooks, task completions, client events ── insert signals in the same transaction ┘
```

The loops talk only through tables and queues, never by calling each other, so any loop can be stopped, restarted or scaled alone.

- **The database is the truth, queue messages are hints.** A lost or duplicated message costs a delay at most: the planner re-finds anything due, and every step re-checks state under a row lock before acting.
- **Transactional outbox.** With `SEQ_QUEUE=postgres` the enqueue is an insert in the same transaction as the state change, so "run recorded" and "message queued" cannot diverge. With Redis, the reaper re-enqueues anything recorded but never queued.
- **Timers are rows, not process timers.** `next_run_at` on the enrollment is the timer; the scheduler wakes the planner every five seconds. Nothing is lost on restart.
- **Logic steps run inline** (wait, condition, end, task creation) in one transaction, up to 25 steps per advance; a `crm.*` condition makes one Attio call inside that step. Only steps with an external side effect (email, SMS) hop to a queue.

## The scheduler

No cron extension: each periodic job has a lease row in `seq.job_locks`, and whichever replica takes the lease runs it. A lease is a few periods long, so a crashed leader is replaced within seconds, and every job is idempotent, so an overlap is harmless. Plain statements only, so a transaction pooler is fine.

| Job | Period | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `plan` | 5 s | claim due enrollments fairly per sender (50 per sender, 500 per tick) and advance them; resume pauses whose `paused_until` passed |
| `signals` | 30 s | process unprocessed signals (the safety net for a lost message) |
| `poll` | 60 s | for every active email channel: Gmail history from the stored cursor (scoped to threads the service started in the last 30 days), Calendar events from its cursor; every 15 minutes a health check that raises `sender.channel_status` on a change |
| `reap` | 60 s | release planner claims older than 10 minutes, re-enqueue runs recorded but never queued, fail runs stuck for 2 hours, re-enqueue deliveries whose next attempt is due |
| `webhooks` | 30 s | sweep deliveries due for a retry |
| `retention` | daily | prune idempotency keys after a day, dead-queue rows and delivered webhook deliveries after 30 days |

## Ownership, and why there is no double send

While an executor works on a step, the enrollment's `owner_run_id` points at that run and `next_run_at` is null, so the planner cannot see it; only the executor that finishes the run hands the enrollment back. Every email carries a `Message-ID` derived from the run's idempotency key, and a retry first asks the provider whether that id already went out (`findSent`) before sending again. A text has no such key, so an unknown outcome searches the last ten minutes of outbound texts to that number, and if that is inconclusive the run goes to `needs_review` instead of being retried: a double text is worse than a late one.

## Signals

Everything that comes from outside becomes a signal row, unique per `(project, source, external id)`, so a webhook delivered twice changes nothing. Processing a signal is, per enrollment it can touch and in its own transaction: attribute (the first reply and the first meeting per enrollment, never rewritten), then exit (an opt-out, a hard bounce, a complaint or a suppression always exits; a reply or a meeting exits when `exit_on` says so), pause (an out-of-office reply pauses until its stated date or seven days), or wake a `wait_for` or a task. The effect is recorded, so a redelivered signal is a no-op. Afterwards `signal.received` is delivered to your endpoints.

## Gates at execution

The planner checks nothing about deliverability; the executor does, at the last moment: the contact's email or phone exists, no suppression on the channel (or on all channels), the channel exists on the sender and is `active`, the daily cap and minimum gap, the sequence's send window, and for SMS also consent, an allowed country (`sms_countries`), a known recipient time zone and 08:00 to 21:00 recipient-local. A gate that cannot pass skips the step (recorded with its reason, the flow takes `skipped`); a gate that will pass later defers it (`send_window`, `daily_limit`, `min_gap`, `quiet_hours`, `kill_switch`, `credentials_needed`), and the enrollment goes back to the planner with a new `next_run_at`.

Read the full design in the [architecture document](https://github.com/lguerberg/open-sequence/blob/main/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md); these docs are for integrating, not for building the engine.
